# ORC-030 stabilization: the backlog and its root causes

This backlog lists 37 known limits and deferred findings for ORC-030 step 2 (stabilization with root-cause analysis), swept from the task specs, the design docs, the real-run records, the safety notes, the README and code comments on `orc-029-pass5` (at 2482345) and `orc-031` (at f4bac9b). Three are high for a first real user: a paused Codex run can stop the whole factory once a building budget is set, the PE never computes its estimate, and the setup path has never run for real; 14 are medium and 16 are low. B-32 and B-33 were found on 2026-10-03, after the sweep. B-39 to B-58 were added on 2026-10-04 from the owner and from ORC-032's reviews and QA. They stay unworked: the backlog stays backlog. After the table come the items already fixed, the items handed to the UI audit, and 9 product decisions for the owner.

**Where it fits.** ORC-030 r4 (`docs/tasks/ORC-030.md:65`) says the backlog starts from every known limit the specs and design docs record. QA (step 1) adds rows from its journeys. Each fix then goes in at its root, with a test that fails first, and the lesson goes into a principle, a check or a spec.

**How to read it.**
- "Still true?" is what I checked in the code on `orc-029-pass5` (or `orc-031` for ORC-031 rows). "Unsure" means I did not find proof either way.
- "Root" is where the mismatch started: intent, design, build, or a check that missed it.
- "Severity" is for a first real user of the app, not for the developers.
- Size: S is under half a day, M is one to two days, L is more. These are guesses with no measured basis.
- Items the owner dropped (ORC-015, ORC-018, ORC-019, ORC-020) are left out.
