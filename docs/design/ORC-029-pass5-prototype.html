Screen 3 · The Lock in summary

Before a Lock in, one screen says what it does, like the pre-flight before Start the factory: what changes, the tasks it touches, the new work, the effect on the budgets, and what stays open. Only you can lock in. Your agreement is recorded with this summary. Then the lead adjusts the tasks (screen 4).

Question 1 · A task is building something you changed

T-012 builds the Trip plan from v3 while you lock in v4. Let it finish: no work is lost, and the lead's update builds the change on top (more total work, no idle agent). Pause it: nothing more is built on the old design, but the work so far may be thrown away, and an agent waits.

Question 2 · All changes, or a choice

The whole draft: one click, and the design stays consistent, because changes often depend on each other. An open item never goes in. A choice: you lock in part of the draft and keep the rest, but the summary must then check that the parts fit together.

Screen 5 · Design and reality: pick a layout

Each part of the design shows where it stands in the factory: designed, in the draft, being built, built and verified, or fails a check. Evidence sits beside the design, so you never look at the code: the built screen beside its prototype, the built CLI's recording beside its demo, and each rule of a flow with its test. Question 3 · When is a part "built and verified"?

The checks decide: each rule of its flows has a passing test, and the UX review finds the built screen matches the approved prototype. You see the evidence, but you need not act. You confirm: the same, and then a "Looks right" from you on each part. That is more certain, but it puts you back in the loop for every part.
