# ORC-007: Fan-out: breakdown steps that create tasks, and parallel agents per step

## Task identity

- ID and title: ORC-007, fan-out at both levels: steps that break work into child tasks, and several agents on one step
- Author and timestamps: Lead (Claude), 2026-09-29
- Status, priority, and priority rationale: Completed, P1. The user asked whether a plan or design step can create multiple tasks, and whether each pipeline step can have parallel agents. Both serve the user's goal of using Claude and Codex capacity at large scale.
- Specification revision: 3

## Problem and outcome

Today only the lead creates tasks, and each step is exactly one agent run. Large goals therefore serialise inside a task, and a design cannot hand its work items straight to parallel execution.

Outcome:

- A step can produce a **task breakdown**. The service turns each valid item into a child task linked to its parent, and the children run concurrently.
- A step can run **several agents at once**:
  - **Independent copies**, whose outputs are combined.
  - **Best of N**, where a later step chooses one of the candidates.

Everything stays visible, pausable, and editable, like any other step.

## Scope

Included:

- **Breakdown artifacts** (a new artifact kind, `breakdown`). Items are validated like lead proposals: title, outcome, acceptance, template, optional dependencies between items. On acceptance, each item becomes a child task (`parentTaskId`, IDs `<parent>.1`, `<parent>.2`, …).
  - The parent's later steps may wait for all children with `waitForChildren`.
  - A review gate on the breakdown step lets the user edit the list before any child exists.
  - A newer version of the same breakdown (a re-run or an edit) reconciles instead of adding a second batch. Children still listed are kept, unstarted children that are no longer listed are cancelled, and started ones are kept and reported (r3).
  - Children follow the same autonomy holds as lead proposals and count toward the lead's open-proposal cap. Pausing or cancelling a task applies to its unfinished children, and resuming it resumes the children that were paused with it (r3).
- **Per-step parallelism:** `parallel: { count: 2–5, mode: "copies" | "best-of", providers? }`. Optional providers alternate across the copies (for example one Claude and one Codex copy).
  - When the step becomes ready, it expands into separate steps `<id>`, `<id>-c2`, …, recorded as a pipeline revision. Each copy is an ordinary step with its own runs, controls, and artifacts.
  - *Copies:* later steps receive every copy's output, and review findings are summed for run-if conditions. Steps that change code cannot use copies, because the copies cannot all be merged (r3).
  - *Best of N:* writers work on separate branches. The first later step that reads the candidates compares them and names one (`chosen`). Other readers wait for the choice, and only the chosen change flows further or is integrated. The others stay on their branches.
  - Each completion of the comparing step decides again. A person may choose or change the candidate. That choice stands until the chosen candidate produces a new run result, and every step that received the old choice is revalidated (r3).
- **Controls:** pause, stop, stale-result, and revalidation rules apply to every copy. Provider limits count every copy.
- **UI:**
  - Pipeline editor fields for parallelism and breakdown.
  - Parent and child links on tasks.
  - Candidate comparison and choice in artifacts.
- **Tests** for the domain and scheduler with scripted adapters.

- **Iteration (user direction, r2: "It should iterate"):**
  - *Step loops.* A step may declare `iterate: { from, max }`, which makes the steps from `from` through it (for example review → repair) a loop.
    - When the loop's last step completes, the service appends the next iteration (`<id>-i2`, …) as a pipeline revision. It stops when that step is skipped (the review reports no open findings) or at `max`, which is at most 10.
    - Built-in templates loop on their repair steps, up to 3 times.
    - If a re-run makes an earlier iteration end clean, the later iterations are skipped (r3).
  - *Goal loops.* After a breakdown's children settle, an evaluation step (the lead, or a designated reviewer) checks the parent's acceptance and either declares the goal met or produces the next breakdown. This repeats up to the parent's iteration budget.
  - Pause, gates, and edits work between any two iterations.

Excluded, and enforced (r3):

- Nested breakdowns more than two levels deep.
- Child tasks using a template that breaks down again.
- More than 20 items per breakdown, or more than 100 child tasks (all levels) per task a person created.
- Parallel steps that create child tasks, parallel steps inside a loop, and overlapping or nested loops.
- A comparing step with a run-if condition or its own parallelism.
- Automatic merging of best-of candidates.

## Options and tradeoffs

| Option | Approach | Benefit | Effort | Risks | Reversibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Breakdown artifact creates child tasks; `parallel` on steps with copies and best-of modes | Fans out at both levels, and everything reuses the existing controls | Large | More runs mean more cost, bounded by limits and caps | High |
| B | Let the lead handle all decomposition (status quo) and only add step copies | Smaller | Medium | Design steps still cannot hand work to parallel execution | High |

## Decision

- Agent recommendation: A
- Selected option: A
- Decided by: Lead
- Rationale and evidence: A answers both of the user's questions and keeps every run inside the same controls: holds, gates, edits, and limits.
- Uncertainty and what would change the decision: if best-of candidates prove hard to compare from summaries, add a diff-summary artifact for each candidate.

## Acceptance and validation

- A breakdown step's valid items become child tasks. Invalid items are rejected with reasons in the parent's activity. Edits at a gate change which children get created.
- A parent step with `waitFor: "children"` starts only after every child has settled.
- Parallel copies run concurrently up to the provider limits. A pause stops every copy. A late result from a superseded copy is discarded.
- In best-of, the chooser's selection is recorded, and only the chosen change reaches later steps and integration.
- Checks: typecheck, tests, build, and a browser pass.

## Completion evidence

**Independent review.** An independent reviewer (a separate agent) checked the implementation with probes that used only domain calls. Every finding was reproduced, then fixed, and each fix has a regression test in `server/fanout.test.ts`:

| Finding | Severity | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A re-run comparison's new choice was ignored | High | Every completion of the comparing step decides again. A person's choice stands until the chosen candidate produces a new run result, and a changed leader re-runs and re-syncs its copies. |
| Children bypassed autonomy and had no total cap | High | Children follow lead-proposal holds and count toward the open cap. There is a cap of 100 per task a person created, and child tasks cannot use breakdown templates. |
| Parallel breakdown steps multiplied children | High | This is now rejected by validation. |
| `setPipeline` could not remove `iterate`, `parallel`, or `waitForChildren` | Medium | The new definition replaces every optional field, so an identical re-save changes nothing. |
| A parallel step inside a loop silently ended the loop | Medium | This is now rejected by validation, as are overlapping or nested loops. |
| Copies of a code change integrated only one copy | Medium | This is now rejected by validation, which points to best of N. |
| A skipped comparing step left no choice | Medium | A comparing step cannot have a condition or its own parallelism. Integration falls back to the first candidate, never "whichever finished last". |
| Pausing or cancelling a parent did not reach its children | Medium | Both now cascade, and resuming the parent resumes what was paused with it. |
| A re-run breakdown added a second batch of children | Medium | Breakdowns reconcile by title, both on re-runs and on edits. |
| Low findings | Low | Fixed: readers besides the comparing step wait for the choice and are revalidated when it changes; a copy on the leader's provider keeps its pinned model; iterations after a re-run that came back clean are skipped; a parent shows "Waiting for N child tasks" and is blocked when a child can never start. Documented only: a parent with no step that waits for its children finishes while they run. |

**Re-verification.** The reviewer re-ran its probes against the fixes. All 13 findings were confirmed resolved, and the round found new defects, now fixed with regression tests:

| Finding | Severity | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A copy sync could store an invalid pipeline | Medium | The synced pipeline is validated again. A change that would break the copies' readers is refused with the reason. |
| An automatic re-choice did not revalidate other readers | Medium | It shares `chooseCandidate`'s revalidation. Readers also wait while the comparing step re-runs (unless a person's choice stands). |
| A best-of step with a condition could deadlock | Medium-low | This is now rejected by validation. |
| Integration could merge a change from a step that was later skipped (pre-existing) | Medium | `finalChange` uses only the accepted outputs of steps that are done now. |
| Changing `parallel` after expansion was ignored | Low | This is now refused. |
| A client omitting `copyOf` or `iteration` broke groups | Low | These fields are service-set: they carry over from the existing step and cannot be added. |
| Grandchildren stayed paused when an intermediate task resumed | Low | Resuming resumes descendants that were paused with the task or with one of its ancestors. |

Design note: child tasks count toward the lead's open-proposal cap, so a large goal paces the lead's other proposals until its children finish.
