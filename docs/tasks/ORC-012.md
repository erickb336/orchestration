# ORC-012: Shape the vision with the lead first

## Task identity

- ID and title: ORC-012, a shaping stage in which the user and the lead shape the vision (and a first roadmap) before any work runs, as an alternative to starting the full pipeline from the beginning
- Author and timestamps: Lead (Claude), 2026-09-30
- Status, priority, and priority rationale: In progress, P1. The user asked: "Can we create a feature where we create a vision with lead before initiating full pipeline vs full pipeline from the beginning".
- Specification revision: 2
- Project vision revision: PROJECT_SPEC.md as of 2026-09-30

## Problem and outcome

Observed:

- **The vision is a text box.** New projects require it filled in (`initProject`), and the lead reads it but cannot draft or change it. ORC-009 lets the lead change only the focus.
- **Work starts at once.** As soon as the lead plans or a task exists, pipelines run: agents are spending tokens before the goal is clear.
- **No way to think it through first.** There is no way to talk the goal through with the lead, see a draft vision and a first plan, and only then start.

Outcome: a project can be in one of two stages.

- **Shaping.** You and the lead talk:
  - The lead asks focused questions and drafts the vision: the problem, who it is for, goals, non-goals, constraints, how you will know it worked, and a first focus.
  - It may also propose a first roadmap of tasks.
  - Nothing runs: no worker steps and no planning runs.
  - You accept, edit or dismiss each draft. The vision stays yours: a draft becomes the vision only when you accept it.
- **Building.** Everything works as today. You move from Shaping to Building with **Start building**, and you can go back to Shaping at any time (running work finishes; nothing new starts).
- **Choosing at the start.** A new project begins by asking which you want: shape the vision with the lead first, or start building now. The second is the current behaviour.

## Scope

Included:

- **Stage.** `Project.stage`, either `"shaping"` or `"building"`. The migration 11 → 12 sets every existing project to building. `initProject` takes an optional stage (default building) and allows an empty vision when shaping.
- **In shaping:**
  - No worker step is dispatched.
  - Planning runs are off.
  - The lead answers your messages as usual.
  - Running work, for example after going back to Shaping, finishes normally.
  - Labels say "Shaping: new work waits until you start building", never "Paused".
- **The lead in shaping is an active partner (r2).** Its message runs get a discovery brief. The lead:
  - **asks targeted questions**, 3–5 per turn, aimed at the most important open areas and grounded in the conversation, the vision documents and the repository, each with a one-line reason;
  - **asks about intent first** (why, for whom, what outcome), then solution ideas;
  - **offers options with a recommendation** for open areas, so you can answer by picking;
  - **suggests what you may not have considered:** users, edge cases, risks, success measures, non-goals, a smaller first milestone;
  - **answers from the code instead of asking** when the repository already answers a question;
  - **restates** what it understood, points out contradictions, and labels assumptions;
  - **keeps a living draft** of the vision from the first useful exchange and improves it each turn, with proposed defaults marked as assumptions;
  - **proposes a first roadmap** once intent and scope are partly clear.
- **Reply fields.** The lead's reply may carry:
  - a `vision` draft (text and focus);
  - `questions`: at most 5, each with its reason and at most 4 options;
  - `coverage` of nine areas, each clear, partial or open: intent and why now, who it is for, problem and today's workaround, outcome and success measures, scope, constraints, risks and unknowns, priorities and first milestone, existing material;
  - proposals.

  All of these are strictly validated, capped and cleaned. Steering (ORC-009) still applies to focus and priorities.
- **Answering in place.** The shaping panel shows the coverage as a checklist, and the questions as a list you answer inline (option chips fill the answer). "Send answers" posts one message with each question and its answer.
- **Vision drafts.**
  - Drafts are stored as suggestions, with the run and your messages that led to them.
  - The UI shows each draft with a diff against the current vision, and offers **Accept**, **Edit and accept**, and **Dismiss**.
  - Accepting creates a vision revision authored by you, which records the draft it came from.
  - A newer draft supersedes an open one.
- **The roadmap.** Proposals made while shaping are planned: they are held and never run while shaping. They are marked as coming from shaping.
- **Start building.**
  - It needs a non-empty vision. If any coverage area is still open, it lists those areas and asks you to confirm, but it never blocks for that.
  - It sets the stage to building.
  - It releases the roadmap according to your involvement setting: on Autopilot the planned tasks start; with check-in or "only when I ask" they wait for you, as lead proposals do today.
- **Back to shaping.** Available at any time; nothing running is stopped.
- **UI:**
  - The Overview's "Shape the vision" panel: what shaping means, the conversation, the latest draft, the planned tasks, and Start building (with the reason when it is disabled).
  - The first step of the Get started list: shape first, or start building now.
  - A stage indicator in the header.
  - The stage control in Settings → Project.
- **Simulated runtime:** a simulated lead drafts a vision from your message, labelled simulated.
- **Tests** with the scripted adapter, and the migration.

Excluded:

- Per-task shaping. A single task can already pause at a gate (for example the Goal template's breakdown).
- The lead editing an accepted vision without you. Drafts are always suggestions.

## Options and tradeoffs

| Option | Approach | User benefit | Effort | Costs and risks | Reversibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Project stage (shaping/building) with vision drafts as suggestions and a held roadmap | Think it through with the lead before spending on work; the vision stays yours; one click to start | Medium | A new concept (stage); lead output grows a `vision` field | High: switch the stage |
| B | Use the existing project pause plus hand-edited vision | No new concept | None | Pausing also stops the lead, so no conversation; no drafts | N/A |
| C | A special "vision" task with a design-only pipeline | Reuses tasks | Medium | Its output is not the vision; planning and dispatch still run elsewhere | Medium |

## Decision

- Agent recommendation: A.
- Selected option: A.
- Decided by: Lead.
- Rationale and evidence: only A lets the lead talk while nothing runs, and keeps the vision the user's own through accept-or-dismiss drafts. It reuses the conversation, steering, held proposals and involvement settings rather than adding a parallel mechanism.
- Uncertainty and what would change the decision:
  - Whether a real lead asks good questions from this brief. This is unverified without a real lead run.
  - Whether new projects should default to shaping. The UI asks every time instead of assuming.

## Acceptance and validation

- In shaping:
  - No worker step dispatches and no planning run starts, while a message is still answered.
  - A draft never changes the vision until it is accepted, and dismissing leaves the vision unchanged.
  - Accepting creates a user-authored revision linked to the draft.
- Start building:
  - It is refused with an empty vision.
  - It releases planned tasks on Autopilot, and keeps them waiting otherwise.
- Going back to shaping stops nothing that is running, and starts nothing new.
- Lead output is validated: a malformed or oversized `vision` is rejected with a reason, and a planning run cannot draft.
- The migration sets existing projects to building.
- Checks: `npm run typecheck`, `npm test`, `npm run build`, and a browser pass on the simulated runtime.
