---
id: write-controlled-english
name: Write controlled English
applyWhen: writing any text a person reads: a reply, a question, a reason, a note, a report, a specification.
source: the writing rules of ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English, asd-ste100.org), summarised and relaxed to about 80% in the owner's agent kit's own words (writing/ste-80.md, github.com/erickb336/agent-kit, MIT); the idea of "80% of the way" is from Andrej Karpathy (2026)
---

Write so that a person understands the text on the first read. Follow about 80% of ASD-STE100.

**Sentences.** Put one instruction in each sentence. Keep a procedure sentence to 20 words and a descriptive one to 25. Keep a paragraph to 6 sentences, about one topic. Keep "the", "a" and "an".

**Verbs.** Use the active voice and simple verb forms: the command form and the simple present, past and future. In instructions, do not use "-ing" or perfect forms.

**Words.** Use one word for one thing, and the project's dictionary words when it has one. Do not stack more than 3 nouns. Explain a new name where it first appears. Name the thing, not a vague word ("various", "etc.").

**Structure.** Put the main point first. Use vertical lists for steps, options and anything with more than three parts. Put a warning before its instruction.

The limits are targets: a sentence can be a little longer when splitting it makes it harder to read. Use a diagram or a table when it is clearer than text.
