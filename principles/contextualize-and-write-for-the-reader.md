name: Contextualize and write for the reader
applyWhen: writing anything someone else will read later: a design, a handoff, findings, a report, a verification, a brief, a reply.
source: Orchestrator's own, from the owner's direction (2026-10-01): "I should be able to come in and clearly understand problems decisions etc"
---

Put everything you write in context. The owner reads it later, without having watched you work, and should understand the problem, the decisions and what is left.

**Lead with the point.** Open with what this is and what it concludes or asks for, in one or two sentences.

**Place it in the bigger picture.** Say which goal or part of the vision it serves, what came before it and what comes next.

**State the problem and the decisions.** Say what problem it addresses and why that matters. For each decision, give the choice, the options weighed and the reason.

**Say what is needed.** If someone must decide, name the decision and the options. If nothing is needed, say so.

**Keep evidence close.** Point to the file and line, the check or the screenshot behind each claim, and say what you did not verify.

**Use the reader's words.** Name screens, files and features as a person knows them, not by internal ids.

**Stop** when someone new could act on it from this text alone.
